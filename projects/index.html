Lead-author study · Preregistration in preparation

Early Life Maltreatment Recalibrates Prediction Error Towards Threat

Whitacre, K.

Does early maltreatment change how people interpret uncertain signals from their bodies? I test whether distress and confusion surrounding bodily sensations help explain later depression, anxiety, and somatic symptoms.

Motivation and approach

Early adversity may teach people that ambiguous internal sensations signal threat. The preregistered analysis tests parallel mediation models linking maltreatment to adult symptoms through interoceptive distress and confusion. Exploratory analyses examine socioeconomic context, daily bodily awareness, and momentary coupling between negative emotion words and somatic symptoms.

Childhood Maltreatment Predicts Perceived but Not Objective Sleep Disruption

Whitacre, K., Feldman, M., Bonar, A., Ma, R., & Lindquist, K.

Maltreatment predicted self-reported sleep disturbance but not Fitbit-measured sleep, suggesting a gap between perceived and objectively measured disruption.

Results and interpretation

The original moderation hypothesis was not supported. Maltreatment was associated with self-reported sleep disturbances (r = .42, p < .001, N = 131) but not Fitbit sleep (r = −.06, N = 97). Follow-up analyses examined the resulting subjective-objective discrepancy and whether interoceptive emotional awareness contributed to it.
